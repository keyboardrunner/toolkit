# Evaluation 01 — Landing Page

## Purpose

Check whether the agent can turn a short product brief into a distinctive, coherent, responsive landing page without inventing unsupported product claims.

## Prompt

> Build a responsive landing page for a fictional task-management app called “Daymark”, aimed at small creative teams. The main promise is to help teams see priorities and next actions without a complicated project-management setup. Include a clear hero, primary CTA, three benefit sections, a realistic product preview, and a final CTA. Use concise copy. Do not invent customer logos, testimonials, statistics, awards, or integrations. Inspect the result at desktop and mobile sizes if the environment supports it.

## Acceptance criteria

- The primary value proposition and CTA are immediately clear.
- Sections form a coherent information hierarchy and narrative.
- The visual direction is intentional and avoids a generic template feel.
- The product preview supports the story and is plausible.
- Copy is specific but contains no fabricated evidence or claims.
- Layout, typography, and spacing adapt to a narrow viewport.
- Interactive controls work or are clearly non-interactive by design.

## Key failure signals

Generic hero copy, arbitrary gradients/decorations, repeated cards with no hierarchy, unsupported metrics, broken mobile layout, and a CTA that does not work.
